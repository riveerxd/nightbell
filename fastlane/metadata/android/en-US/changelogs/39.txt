Checking for an update no longer tells GitHub anything. Nightbell asks its own site which version is current, so the one request it makes on your behalf goes to the people who wrote it rather than to a stranger. GitHub is still in Settings if you would rather it was asked instead.

That request is also the only way this app can know how many people use it. The site's log holds a timestamp, a status and the version string the app sends, and nothing else: no address, and no identifier that could join one day of yours to the next. A fresh install says the word "new" once and never again, and counting that word is what makes the number exact without anything having to be recognised.

The screen that asks for permission to page you is reachable again. It showed once, and tapping past it set a flag that told it never to come back, so a phone that had never granted the full screen permission could not be woken by an urgent alert and nothing anywhere said why. It comes back now while a permission it needs is missing, and Settings, Alerts, Alert permissions opens it on purpose. Reached that way it looks like the rest of Settings and skips the first run copy, and its colours mean what they mean elsewhere: rose is the notifications row alone, because that is the one whose absence delivers nothing at all.

A page monitor stalled on a request that already failed stops telling you to raise the timeout. The renderer reports itself most of the way loaded, so the advice looked reasonable and could not work: taking sixty seconds to ninety does not resolve a hostname a firewall is blocking. Both the paragraph and the amber line above it now lead with the failed request, and the paragraph is no longer cut half a sentence short of saying where the diagnostic log lives.

Every address this app chose for itself, the new one included, is pinned to HTTPS and the certificates the phone shipped with. Your monitors are untouched and still trust whatever you have told the phone to trust, because a NAS behind a private certificate authority is half of what people watch.
