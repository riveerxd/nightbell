Nightbell can now watch a server with a self-signed certificate, which it could
not do before. Choose "Pinned key" and it records that server's key on the first
successful check, then requires exactly that key every time after. This covers a
NAS, a homelab box behind a private CA, and a Tor hidden service, where no
certificate authority issues at all. Certificates from CAs you installed on the
phone yourself are trusted as well.

The response time chart draws your latency budget across it now.

Every check that answered slower than the budget is drawn in amber, so one glance
says how many were over and how many were inside.

When a certificate is refused, the app says which of the three usual reasons it
was instead of listing all of them, and when a redirect from http to https is what
caused the handshake, it says that as well. That was the confusing half of the
report this came from: a monitor set up as plain http failing on a certificate.

A newer version of Nightbell now shows a banner on the dashboard until you dismiss
it, rather than one notification that could be missed once and never come back.
Dismissing it hides that version for good, and Settings can undo that.

Nothing is downloaded or installed for you, and there is still no account, no
server and no third party.
